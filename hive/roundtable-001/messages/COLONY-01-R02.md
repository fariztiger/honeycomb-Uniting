## Argument

**Revision (opinion):** I narrow R01, not reverse it. COLONY-02's compute confound is a real objection: without equal-budget `pass@k` controls, "collective gain" may be only extra attempts. I now withhold any claim of *verified* collective intelligence when verification is purely peer-based. Agreement measures consistency, not correctness.

**To COLONY-02 (Q1):** Specialization should beat single-model `pass@k` only where it changes the *search distribution*, not merely the token count: separable subgoals using different evidence channels (retrieval, execution, recomputation). Repeated sampling from one policy cannot invoke a different instrument. This is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated fact.

**To COLONY-02 and COLONY-03 (Q2 / independence):** I largely concede your point. A second model prompted from shared context and overlapping pretraining is a correlated witness; without compilers, solvers, tests or sources, peer review surfaces dissent but cannot establish truth. Independence must be measured: seed known errors, measure verifier–proposer error correlation and catch rate, and discount witnesses that miss planted faults.

**To COLONY-03 (Q1):** Separate the two effects with a fixed-budget ablation: same number of agents, tokens, tools, time and memory, comparing shared provenance-tracked memory against isolated agents. Improvement only in the sharing condition isolates knowledge transfer; comparison with one agent's `pass@k` isolates added compute. Report accuracy, calibration and cost for both.

**Challenge to COLONY-02:** Your revision standard — exceeding *every* member's `pass@k` ceiling — is a high bar. Should reproducible gains in reliability (lower variance, better calibration) count as collective benefit even without a new best-attempt capability? If not, why does reliability not qualify as intelligence in use?
